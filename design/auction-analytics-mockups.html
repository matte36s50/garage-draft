Which way is the market moving?

Average sale price can't answer this — it moves when the mix of cars changes, not when values do. This board uses a repeat-sale index built from buckets that sold in both periods, so a quarter full of Ferraris doesn't read as a rising market.

Are the estimates right, and where are they wrong?

You store estimate_low / estimate_high for catalogue lots and price_at_48h for BaT. Both are a prior; the sale is the posterior. The gap between them, sliced by marque and by house, is where the tradeable information lives.

Does attention turn into money?

You collect watchers, views, comments and bid_count but nothing currently reads them. BaT and Cars & Bids publish no estimate, so the outcome here is price ÷ the lot's own bucket median, never price ÷ estimate — that keeps online and live lots on one scale and needs no catalogue.

Does online attention price the room at Monterey?

The unit here is the bucket, not the lot — a Monterey lot has no watcher count, so the only way to connect the two markets is through canonical_model_id. Online attention for a model is measured on BaT and Cars & Bids; the outcome is what that same model does at premier live sales.

What I deliberately left out

You asked for nothing that doesn't add insight. These are the things I removed, and the reason each one fails — several are on the current analytics page.

Cut · misleading

Average estimate & average final price

Auction prices are log-normal with a fat right tail. One $2.4M Gullwing moves the mean of a 400-lot quarter by more than the other 399 combined. Medians and percentiles replace them; the "median vs avg gap" tile that currently exists is a workaround for a number that shouldn't be on the page.

Cut · unreadable

Estimate vs final, 30 most recent, grouped bars

Sixty bars with 40°-rotated labels, ordered by nothing anyone cares about. It answers "what happened to lot #17" — a question the sortable table answers better — while looking like it answers something aggregate. The distribution histogram on board 02 is the aggregate version.

Cut · geometry

Linear-axis estimate-vs-final scatter

With a $9k Miata and a $2.4M Gullwing on the same linear axis, 90% of points compress into the bottom-left corner. Log-log fixes it, but the ratio histogram answers the same question without asking anyone to read a scatter.

Cut · mix effect

Absolute-dollar price brackets

"$25k–$50k" pools a running Miata with a project Ferrari. Sell-through by absolute bracket therefore measures which segments happened to consign that quarter. Board 01 uses value tiers on a repeat-sale basis; board 03 ranks within band.

Cut · forbidden

Price and sell-through on one dual-axis chart

The obvious way to show board 01's story, and it invents a correlation — the alignment of the two y-scales is arbitrary. Two charts stacked on a shared x-axis show the same lead-lag relationship without the artefact.

Cut · no such field

Realized-vs-estimate on BaT and Cars & Bids

Neither publishes a pre-sale estimate, so an estimate ratio can only ever be computed for catalogue houses — board 02 is scoped to those 957 lots and says so. Everywhere online lots appear (boards 03 and 05) the outcome is price ÷ the lot's own bucket median instead, which needs no catalogue and puts online and live lots on one scale.

Cut · wrong unit

Lot-level online → live regression

A Monterey lot has no watcher count — engagement exists only on the online side, so there is no lot-to-lot join. Board 05 aggregates to bucket × quarter, which is the finest grain at which both markets are actually observed.

Cut · thin data

Any segment with n<8

A make with three sales produces a headline "+180% vs estimate" that is one car. Thin segments are withheld and counted in a footnote rather than plotted, everywhere on this page.
